\begin{abstract}
We study graph homomorphisms from trees to fixed target graphs $H$, focusing
on Leontovich graphs: targets for which the path does not minimize the number
of homomorphisms. We prove that no $T(x,y,z)$ has a near-path crossover at
$n=5$. At $n=6$, for targets in this family, four of the six source-tree
comparisons are proved; the remaining two reduce to explicit polynomial
inequalities and remain open beyond the tested parameter range. The smallest
crossover threshold witnessed here is $n=7$. For symmetric trees with two or
three orbits, an odd near-path crossover can be decided by a single finite
sign check. For the four-orbit tree $T(7,1,9)$, we prove that its odd threshold
$n=13$ is permanent. We also prove that the bipartite double cover preserves
the Leontovich property. Our searches produce a 15-vertex witness $H^*$ by
exhaustive bipartite enumeration and an 18-vertex witness $H_{18}$ by SMT
synthesis. The latter first crosses at $(n,d)=(15,4)$ and is also minimal in
the completed depth-2 sweep through $n \le 51$. Finally, a
3,644-vertex double-cover witness first crosses at the even threshold
$n=17{,}340$, disproving the proposed bipartite parity hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
