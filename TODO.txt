• Double-cover identity

  - The paper states the identity for a connected tree T:
    \[
      \Hom(T,B)=2\Hom(T,H).
    \]
  - The Lean theorem `connected_hom_doubleCover_eq_two_mul` is broader: it applies to any graph `G` with `ConnectedBipartiteColoring G`.
  - Keep the paper wording narrow and treat the Lean theorem as the formalized generalization.

• Near-path sufficiency

  - This remains open in the paper.
  - The current analytic obstruction is the long-spider family: if all three legs go to infinity, that challenger does not beat the path.
  - That rules out the easiest non-near-path family, but it does not settle bounded gadgets near an endpoint or in the interior.
  - Plausible proof route: prove asymptotic factorization for rooted gadgets on primitive targets, reduce challengers to a rooted gadget score, then show the minimum is attained by a near-path gadget.

• Analytic vs computational status

  - Likely analytically provable:
    - `m1 = 2` base case.
    - Double-cover lemma.
    - `B(x,y)` spectral family.
    - `H18` walk-table margin, as exact finite arithmetic from the quotient matrix.
  - Probably analytic but still requires work:
    - Strongness of `T^(1,28,1,36)`.
    - `H*` as a 15-vertex Leontovich graph.
    - The `n = 17340` even crossover threshold.
  - Probably computational:
    - `No graph on m <= 11 is Leontovich`.
    - `No smaller bipartite depth-2 graph exists`.
    - `76`-vertex leaf-pruning minimality.
    - The perturbed non-bipartite construction, unless the quotient proof is made fully symbolic.

• Paper strategy

  - State finite exact computations as certified arithmetic lemmas.
  - Reserve `theorem` for claims whose quantifiers are fully covered by proof or exhaustive certificate.
